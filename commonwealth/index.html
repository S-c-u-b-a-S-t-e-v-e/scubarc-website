SCUBARC GENESIS

VIRGINIANS PARTICIPATING IN RESEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE.

Genesis tests a simple idea: can ordinary people contribute something measurable to a governed research system using only a browser — first through bounded compute and participation, and later through carefully designed research-data tasks?

What we are testing

Two ways are live today. A third is the next experiment.

Genesis begins with the parts we can test honestly right now: whether people will participate, whether a normal browser can execute bounded work, and whether returned results can be verified before they count. The next step is to work with an academic partner and determine whether the same participation layer can support a scientifically legitimate public-data task.

LIVE NOW

Contribute Compute

Your browser runs one bounded, harmless synthetic work unit. You give explicit consent, the work executes locally, and only a constrained result receipt is returned for server-side verification. No arbitrary file uploads, no shell access, and no access to personal files.

LIVE NOW

Play Commonwealth Surf

The game is our low-friction participation test. It helps us measure whether people will voluntarily engage with the system while the server keeps authoritative scoring and the research infrastructure remains governed.

NEXT TEST

Help Solve Research Data

With an academic partner, a future round could pair game-like participation with bounded classification, review, or other public-data tasks. That scientific workload is not active today; proving whether it is useful is the purpose of the proposed next experiment.

Why participation matters

Institutional compute is one layer. People can be another.

Virginia's universities, research institutions, and cloud/HPC resources can provide serious research capacity. Genesis explores a complementary bottom-up layer: citizens, classrooms, community organizations, and otherwise idle browsers contributing bounded compute or carefully reviewed human effort where a researcher decides it is appropriate.

Try it now: run one work unit, play one round, inspect the leaderboard, and tell us what breaks. Participation today helps us test the infrastructure before anyone asks it to perform real science.

Built, tested, bounded

The prototype is live. The claims remain intentionally narrow.

Genesis uses server-authoritative results, bounded work envelopes, independent build/admission verification, adversarial timing and security testing, preview/production separation, rollback controls, real-device QA, and a formal post-mission lessons-learned process. The current compute workload is synthetic and deterministic. Genesis does not yet establish scientific usefulness, statewide scale, K–12 suitability, or improved research outcomes.

Naming note: This ScubaRC pilot was initially published under the working name Commonwealth.ai. After recognizing the similarity to the Commonwealth AI Initiative (CAII), ScubaRC adopted the name ScubaRC Genesis to avoid confusion. This pilot is independent and does not represent CAII, SURA, any university, or a Commonwealth of Virginia program.
