# ScubaRC Genesis — CAII Outreach Packet

## Purpose

This packet supports technical outreach to Ryan Wright and Virginia CAII / NSF 26-513 planning participants. It is not a sales brochure, endorsement claim, or competing statewide proposal.

## Working title

**SCUBARC GENESIS — Community Compute & Human Participation Pilot**

Subhead: **A deployed experiment testing whether ordinary browsers and people can become a governed participation layer for research infrastructure.**

Prototype: https://scubaresearchcollective.com/commonwealth/

## Core positioning

Following the Virginia AI infrastructure breakout, Scuba Research Collective built and deployed a working public prototype instead of asking the group to evaluate the concept only on paper.

Genesis demonstrates infrastructure feasibility: a public browser can enroll with low friction, bounded work can be issued and executed locally, results can be returned and verified, participation can be measured, and authoritative state can remain server-side.

The current workload is synthetic and deterministic. Genesis does **not** yet establish scientific usefulness, meaningful research throughput, statewide scalability, K–12 suitability, quality of citizen scientific contribution, or improved scientific outcomes.

## Relationship to CAII

Genesis is independent of CAII and is not a competing statewide initiative. The hypothesis is that Virginia may ultimately need both a top-down institutional infrastructure layer and mechanisms through which citizens, classrooms, community organizations, smaller institutions, and edge/browser resources can participate in appropriate workloads under institutional research authority.

### Trust boundaries

- VOLUNTEER DEVICE != TRUSTED DEVICE
- RETURNED RESULT != AUTHORITATIVE RESULT
- COMPUTE CONTRIBUTED != AUTHORITY GRANTED

## Engineering discipline

Genesis uses:

- immutable release candidates;
- independent build/admission verification;
- server-authoritative result handling;
- adversarial timing and security testing;
- preview/production separation;
- rollback controls;
- real-device QA;
- post-mission lessons-learned archives.

## Proposed 30-day experiment

**DON'T ADOPT IT. TEST IT.**

Give ScubaRC:

- one academic partner;
- one bounded public-data research problem;
- 30 days.

ScubaRC will replace the synthetic workload with an authorized scientifically legitimate task and evaluate workload suitability, correctness, verification, contribution quality, participation, throughput, operational cost, reproducibility, security constraints, failure modes, and researcher usefulness.

The deliverable is a technical report showing whether the model provides useful research contribution, no useful contribution, or only works within specific boundaries. A negative result is a valid outcome.

> We are not asking CAII to assume this model works. Give us one bounded research problem and let the evidence decide.

## Naming note

The public prototype was initially published under the working name **Commonwealth.ai**. After recognizing the similarity to the Commonwealth AI Initiative (CAII), ScubaRC adopted **ScubaRC Genesis** to avoid confusion. The prototype is independent and does not represent CAII, SURA, a university, NSF, Jefferson Lab, or the Commonwealth of Virginia.

## Future direction — deliberately out of scope for the 30-day test

If Genesis proves useful, the same participation architecture could later be evaluated for educator-controlled STEM experiences involving genuine, age-appropriate research workflows.

ScubaRC is separately developing governed AI research-workflow capabilities that could eventually connect verified contributions to researcher-directed missions. That work is outside the scope of the initial Genesis experiment.

## Claims we must not make

- Genesis is currently performing scientific computation.
- Commonwealth Surf currently sorts or labels scientific data.
- Human decisions currently improve research scheduling.
- Genesis has demonstrated scientific value.
- Genesis has demonstrated statewide scalability.
- Community participation has been validated at meaningful scale.
- Genesis is a CAII capability.
- ScubaRC represents CAII, Virginia, SURA, NSF, Jefferson Lab, or participating universities.
- CAII requires ScubaRC.
- The system is suitable for K–12 use today.
- Volunteer results can be trusted without verification.
- ScubaRC's separate governed research-workflow work is already the CAII mission layer.
- A 30-day experiment will produce a useful scientific result.
